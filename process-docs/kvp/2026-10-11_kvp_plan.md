# KVP plan for the ad hoc lane and the deep dive lane (2026-10-11)

KVP (continuous improvement) for websearch means one closed loop: the agents use the tool, the logs record facts, an offline report turns the facts into a few indicators, the weakest indicator gets exactly one fix, and the same report run on the post-fix window shows whether it improved. This entry holds the inventory the plan is built on, the plan, and how it compares to Google SRE practice. Nothing in it is implemented as of 2026-10-11.

## Constraint that shapes everything

The production logs deliberately hold facts, not verdicts. `outcome` was removed from scrape and pipe records on 2026-09-06 (see area `scrape_pipeline`), and since 2026-09-15 the agent sees only the body of a scrape. Every verdict (blocked, usable, relevant) is therefore computed offline by the KVP report, never written back into the production log.

## Silent failures in the real logs

A silent failure here is a call that exited 0 with success-shaped output while the content was unusable.

- Ad hoc scrapes: 23 of 156 (14.7 %). Anti-bot pages under 403 (cosdna.com 506 B, stern.de 121 B, forums.raspberrypi.com 522 B), challenge or interstitial pages under 200 (mojeek "Verification required" 185 B six times, amazon.de 374 B), pages filtered to 1 byte (3), 404 pages (7), login walls after redirect (3). The inverse also exists: 5 scrapes flagged `crawl4ai_success=false` carry real content (stackoverflow.com, superuser.com, eclecticlight.co).
- Search: 229 of 1980 engine runs were captcha, proof-of-work or HTTP 429 pages recorded as `EMPTY` (yandex 126, brave 75, mojeek 18, openalex 10). At least one such engine hit 172 of 262 searches (65.6 %). The agent sees only "0". Google returned `EMPTY` in 114 of 120 runs between 2026-09-06 and 2026-09-18 and nobody noticed for 12 days (cause unverified).
- Deep dive: the pipe log shows 363 of 363 URLs as fine. In `websearch-pages` there are 13 platform.claude.com files consisting of bare "Loading..." lines, 3 duplicate pairs, and one 301 page stored under the child URL.

Every documented silent failure so far was found by a human reading output.

## Plan

### Infrastructure milestones

- **M1 KVP report script** in `dev/kvp/`. Reads the logs, sidecars, cache, rag-cli logs and transcripts; derives the verdicts offline; writes one markdown report per run into `dev/kvp/md/`. It is the single place where indicator definitions live, so before and after are always computed the same way.
- **M2 Code version per record.** Add the git sha to query, scrape and pipe records. Without it a before/after split depends on timestamps and guesswork; the engine set changed on 2026-09-17 with no marker.
- **M3 Deep dive run record.** One jsonl line per deep dive with the run facts listed above, written by the commands the worker already calls, so the lane becomes measurable without reading /tmp.
- **M4 Canary set.** A fixed list of queries and URLs with known expected results, including the observed failure cases (cosdna.com, stern.de, a mojeek verification page, a platform.claude.com "Loading..." page). Run on demand before and after every fix. This replaces alerting, which is pointless at 4.5 scrapes per day.

### Indicators the report computes

| Lane | Level | Indicator |
|---|---|---|
| Ad hoc | Technical | Searches with at least 3 engines OK; block rate per engine (EMPTY with a block signal); usable-body rate of scrapes |
| Ad hoc | Relevance | Share of searches that lead to a scrape; rank of the scraped URL in the drilldown list (click data as implicit relevance, Croft, Metzler, Strohman 2010, ch. 8.3, in `websearch-reference`) |
| Ad hoc | Usefulness | Share of transcript episodes that end with the gap closed (judged from the transcript; method open, hypothesis: an LLM judge) |
| Deep dive | Technical | Discovered, kept, scraped, failed, indexed per run |
| Deep dive | Relevance | Kept share per run; placeholder and duplicate files in the collection |
| Deep dive | Usefulness | Share of indexed documents per domain that ever appear in a rag-cli hit |

### The loop

1. Run the report.
2. Pick the indicator with the largest gap.
3. Make exactly one change.
4. Run the canaries before and after; on paired canary results use a sign test or Wilcoxon test (both had the most power in Parapar et al., `rag-cli-reference`).
5. Run the report again on the post-change window.
6. Write a short process-docs entry with trigger, root-cause class and the before/after numbers.

### First loop candidates from the data

- Blocks shown as "0" in `search_web` (65.6 % of searches affected).
- Silent ad hoc scrape failures (14.7 %).
- `websearch-pages` is never searched; a rule change putting RAG first is proposed as a patch in `dev/kvp/patch/workers_rag_first.patch` (observed: 31 of 283 rag-cli searches went to a reference collection between 2026-09-18 and 2026-10-10).

## Comparison with Google SRE practice

Source: the sre.google deep dive of 2026-10-11 plus a scraping-observability article (blog.crawlex.net).

Where the plan follows SRE:
- An SLI is a ratio of good events to all events; all numerators and denominators already exist in the logs.
- The SRE book counts "HTTP 200 with wrong content" as an error. The silent failures above are exactly that class.
- Postmortems with a trigger and root-cause taxonomy enable trend analysis (workbook appendix C). Step 6 of the loop is a light version of it.
- For low-traffic services the workbook recommends synthetic traffic and longer windows instead of fast alerts. M4 is that.
- The incident-metrics report (sre.google) finds mean time to repair poorly suited for decisions. The plan uses no time-to-fix metrics; N is too small anyway.

Where the plan deliberately deviates:
- No SLO targets and no error budget yet. The SRE advice is not to derive targets from current performance; a target set today would lock in yandex at 43 % and google at 50 % OK. Targets come after two report runs.
- No burn-rate alerting. At 4.5 scrapes per day one failed scrape is a 1000x burn in a one-hour window.
- Verdicts stay out of the production log, following the project's fact-not-verdict decision.

What the crawlex article adds beyond SRE: split transport errors from blocks, track "yield" (useful results per attempt) next to the success rate, watch the body-size distribution, keep canaries, and diff runs. Wall time is no block signal here: block pages took as long as real pages (cosdna 9.6 s, stern 9.1 s), because the browser waits a fixed time.

## Orchestrator record of the first KVP session (2026-10-14)

The owner handed KVP over as a continuous, fully autonomous development stream, decoupled from the iterative-dev skills (doccheck, refactor). It is not meant to finish; each session runs as many iterations as make sense. Worker-level details of every step are in the worker entry of this area dated 2026-10-14.

M3 (deep dive run record) is not built.

### Why these iterations, in this order

- Iteration 1 was the only silent-failure class that is a tool defect and reproducible on demand (bgn page, 1 B of 16742 B). 404 pages and login walls are wrong URL choices, not tool defects.
- Iteration 2 came from the collapse searches (fewer than 3 engines OK). Saturation was the missing golden signal, so instrumentation came before any fix.
- Iteration 3 targets the engine agents depend on most: 42 of 102 attributed ad hoc scrapes came from google drilldowns, and google failed in 16 of 139 searches since 2026-09-19.

### Yandex: watch condition met, deliberately not acted on

The 2026-09-18 decision in area `engine_expansion` kept yandex untouched with a monthly manual captcha click and named the trigger for reconsidering: yandex failing well before the `spravka` cookie expires. The cookie in `~/.websearch/browser-session-selflaunch` expires 2026-10-18/19, yet from 2026-10-11 to 2026-10-14 9 of 10 yandex runs in the production log were redirected to `showcaptcha` and the tenth timed out. Even right after the click, blocks came in bursts (2026-09-22 18:57Z: 9 consecutive), and block share fell with the gap to the previous yandex request (under 1 min: 27 of 65 blocked; over 30 min: 5 of 33). Not acted on because agents never drilled into yandex: 0 of 102 attributed scrapes. A fix would buy no measurable agent benefit. Revisit only if the report starts showing yandex drilldowns.

### Incident caused by measurement

The iteration 3 probe sent about 57 google searches in 20 minutes. Google then served `/sorry/` (HTTP 429) to this IP, which also hit agent searches. Checks at 17:55Z and 17:59Z and two later single searches on 2026-10-14 still got `/sorry/`; in the last one mojeek also answered 403 and duckduckgo `ERR_CONNECTION_RESET`. The iteration 3 verification is therefore open. It is pre-registered in the worker entry and is the first step of the next session. Every probe against a search engine needs a volume budget and a stop at the first block page.
